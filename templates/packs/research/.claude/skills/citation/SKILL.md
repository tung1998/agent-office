# Citation and verification

## Citing
- Each factual sentence ends with its citation `[n]`; the list at the end: publisher, title, published date, accessed date, link, grade.
- Quote only what the source says; a paraphrase keeps its hedges ("up to", "estimated", "in the sample").
- A number keeps its unit, its period and the source's definition (e.g. "unemployment, ILO definition, Q2 2026").

## Verifying a claim
1. Open the source (not a summary of it). Does it say this, not more, not less?
2. Same number, unit, period and definition?
3. Still current: newer data, a correction, a retraction?
4. Is the publisher what it claims to be; primary or reporting someone else?
5. Is there a strong source against it the report missed?

Report: PASS or FAIL on the first line; then each problem: the claim, what the source says (quoted), the fix.

## Fact-check verdicts
| Verdict | Means |
|---|---|
| true | The origin and independent sources support it as stated |
| mostly-true | Right in substance, a detail off (rounding, date) |
| misleading | Technically right but missing context that changes its meaning |
| false | The evidence contradicts it |
| unverifiable | No origin or evidence can be found |
